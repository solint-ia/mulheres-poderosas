## Audience
Primary audience is women interested in entrepreneurship, business growth, leadership, health, aesthetics, technology, personal development, networking and financial independence. Avoid treating them as a generic category. Speak to women who already have ambition, dreams, insecurities and a desire for transformation, the visitor should think "this event was made for me".

Pain points: exhaustion from juggling business, family and self care, decision making loneliness, feeling stuck despite ambition, wanting to grow but lacking references, wanting to invest in herself, wanting to meet other women building something.
Desired feelings: belonging, high value networking, access to health, aesthetic and tech/AI innovation without losing a human touch, confidence, permission to grow without doing everything alone.
Core value proposition line (Portuguese, keep as is): "Crescer sem adoecer. Faturar mais sem perder a sua paz."

## Consultative Mandate
Act as a creative director and product designer, not an order taker. Before and during the build, be critical. If a decision weakens the brand identity, hurts conversion, makes the page feel generic, overloads it with information, creates conflict between the event and organizer logos, undersells the ticket price perception, feels dated or template like, or fails to speak to the female audience, flag it and propose a better alternative. Do not silently follow this brief if a stronger path is visible.

## Emotional Arc To Deliver
Femininity, sophistication, warmth, empowerment, exclusivity and celebration, balanced so the page never reads as childish, corporate cold, or like a generic beauty salon site. The visitor should move through three feelings while scrolling: "isso fala comigo" (identification), "eu quero viver isso" (desire), "eu vou" (decision).

## Design System

### Color palette
Base: off white / cream, warm and slightly floral, #FDFBF7 or #FAF5EF
Primary emotion: magenta / wine, #8B1E3F or #A3284E, plus a soft nude blush #E8D5CE
Premium accent: noble gold #D4AF37, used sparingly for VIP badges, borders and highlights, never as a dominant gradient
Institutional secondary: deep emerald green #1B4332, reserved for the organizer section, footer and credibility seals, for organic contrast
Keep saturation restrained overall, the page must not read as an oversaturated pink or gold festival.

### Typography
Display / headings: Playfair Display or Cormorant Garamond
Body / UI / buttons / prices: Plus Jakarta Sans or Inter
Together they should read editorial, premium, feminine and contemporary, avoid decorative or hard to read fonts.

### Botanical / floral language
Treat floral elements as part of the identity, not as decoration. Favor very subtle petals, thin organic lines, botanical shapes, fine gold ornaments, soft curves and delicate textures, for example flowers emerging from section edges, thin botanical dividers, small gold sparkles around titles. Rule of thumb: if the visitor notices the decoration before the content, there is too much of it. Never use generic stock floral backgrounds.

### Tone of voice
Feminine, confident, inspiring, welcoming, elegant, intelligent, aspirational, human. Avoid generic motivational phrases, excessive emojis, coach style language, cliches, exaggerated promises and long text blocks. The copy should read like a professional major event brand, not an improvised promo page.

### Avoid list
Generic event landing page look, excess pink, childish or overly girly aesthetics, excess gradients, excess gold, excess shadows, generic cards, random icons, cliche stock photos, excess emojis, huge text blocks, template feel, corporate congress site feel, beauty salon site feel, elements competing with each other, a traditional price comparison table that feels like e commerce.

## Content Rules

If no real testimonials are supplied, do not fabricate any, propose an alternative trust building section instead (for example organizer track record, speaker credentials, visual proof of scale).

## Conversion UX Flow
Attention, identification, desire, value, proof, experience, ticket, decision, purchase. Do not scatter CTAs randomly, place them at strategic points. Primary CTA everywhere: Garantir Meu Ingresso, always linking to Sympla. Secondary CTAs, used sparingly: Conhecer o Evento, Ver Experiências, Conhecer os Ingressos.

## Responsiveness
Mobile first, not a compressed desktop copy. Mobile: comfortable reading, large CTAs, simple navigation, stacked cards, extremely clear prices, no decorative elements that hurt legibility. Desktop: editorial composition, larger imagery, overlapping elements, asymmetry, negative space, floral details, expressive typography.

## Microinteractions
Soft hovers, small transitions, gentle entrance animations, subtle organic movement, occasional petal or floral motion, card highlights, CTA transitions. Sophistication over quantity, avoid anything that reads like a generic Webflow template.

## Accessibility and performance
Maintain contrast, legibility, clear focus states, alt text, semantic hierarchy and clearly identifiable buttons at every step, visual sophistication must never break usability. Design with optimizable images, no unnecessary heavy video, no excessive animation, and reusable components in mind, since the final build target is Next.js.

## Technical Output
Deliver clean React / Next.js components (Hero.tsx, Speakers.tsx, Schedule.tsx, Pricing.tsx, Footer.tsx, etc), Tailwind CSS for styling, Framer Motion for fade in up entrance animation, Lucide icons. All Sympla buttons must use a single configurable [LINK_SYMPLA] placeholder. Apply the frontend design skill guidance to avoid a generic AI generated template look. Package the result as a standalone HTML / ZIP export suitable for later integration into a Next.js project (Antigravity). Proposed component tree: LandingPage contains Header, Hero, EventIntroduction, EventPillars, AudienceSection, ExperienceSection, SpeakersSection, TicketSection (EssentialTicket, SignatureTicket, LegacyTicket), InvestmentSection, VenueSection, OrganizerSection, FAQSection, FinalCTA, Footer, StickyMobileCTA. Adjust this tree if a stronger structure is found.

## Process To Follow
1. Diagnosis: review the logos, PDF, copy, audience and pricing hierarchy, summarize key conclusions before designing.
2. Creative direction: propose the visual concept, mood, palette, typography, photographic treatment and floral language, if more than one direction is viable, present two or three and recommend one.
3. Information architecture: confirm or adjust section order, explain briefly why.
4. Wireframe: define page composition before visual polish.
5. Visual design: apply typography, color, imagery, floral elements, logos, cards, CTAs, spacing and editorial composition.
6. Critical review before final delivery, ask: does this feel like a premium event, does the first fold sell the event on its own, will a first time visitor immediately understand why she should attend, do the tickets feel worth the price, does Legacy feel genuinely exclusive, is there information overload, is the floral language elegant or overdone, does the organizer's green identity compete with the event brand, does the site feel custom made or templated. Fix what falls short.

## Guiding Principle
We are not selling a R$300, R$1.000 or R$4.000 ticket. We are selling the feeling that this woman has a reserved moment to invest in herself, discover new possibilities, meet people, learn from great professionals and leave seeing new paths for her own life. The design needs to make that promise felt before it is explained.
